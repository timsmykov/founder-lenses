# Necessity before automation

Help the user decide whether to retain, simplify, redesign, or automate a specific process. This is an editorial synthesis informed by the Founder Systems research, not a shared procedure prescribed by the founders or a guarantee of business outcomes. See [source notes](references/sources.md) for the evidence and limits.

1. Identify the actual outcome, beneficiary, current steps, owner, volume, failure costs and source evidence. If unknown, mark them unknown; inspect available artifacts rather than assuming the workflow exists.
2. For each material step, ask what requirement or outcome it serves and who owns it. Separate externally binding obligations from internal choices. Identify who would be affected if the step were removed, simplified or delayed. If necessity is unresolved, propose a bounded test or ask the responsible owner; never presume deletion is authorized.
3. For necessary work, inspect variation, edge cases, review needs, error recovery and whether quality can be observed. A repeatable task may be a candidate for rules, models or manual support; not all repeatability warrants automation.
4. Compare keeping the current process, simplifying it and a narrow automation. Include setup and maintenance effort, ongoing human work, error and recovery costs, and evidence gaps. Never invent savings, volumes or accuracy.
5. Recommend one reversible next step with an owner, evidence to collect, stop or review condition and a concrete decision point. Preserve human oversight and relevant obligations; do not change live systems, contact people, remove records or automate anything without authority.

Return a concise decision with the reason for each retained or changed step, assumptions, alternatives, tradeoffs and what would change the recommendation. Clearly separate source-supported ideas from your own application to the user's context.
